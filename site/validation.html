Checked against Europe's official warnings

The European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) publishes a ranked table of the flight information regions (FIRs) most affected by interference with GNSS, satellite navigation that includes GPS. Its page says the table is built from the trust score aircraft broadcast with their position (NIC, the Navigation Integrity Category), the same report this map uses. So the two can be compared, FIR by FIR.

The archive finds 94% of EASA's top 20

For each of 20 EASA tables, from May 2025 to November 2026, the archive ranked every FIR it could see by its own share of degraded aircraft over the same days. It kept as many FIRs as EASA listed. Then it counted how many of EASA's top 20 were in that list.

The pass mark was 70%, written down and committed before the comparison ran. A list drawn at random would score about 29%. Only FIRs the archive has enough aircraft to score count; the rest are no data.

It does not match EASA's order or its edges

  • About half the archive's list is not in EASA's table. A list that long reaches down to airspace where only 2 to 3% of aircraft report degraded integrity. Several central European FIRs sit there in almost every table. That level is background in this measure, and EASA's table leaves those FIRs out.
  • The order differs. Within FIRs on both lists, rank agreement is weak: a median Spearman correlation of 0.205, where 1 would mean the same order and 0 no relation. It falls below zero in three 2026 tables.
  • Some of EASA's top FIRs are out of reach. Baghdad, Baku, Lahore and several Russian, Kazakh and East African FIRs had too little traffic to score in some tables.

Tallinn was named 388 days after the interference began

Before May 2025, EASA named affected FIRs in lists: in its bulletin of February 2023, a revision in November 2023, and a web list dated 5 July 2024. The archive shows persistent interference in the Tallinn FIR from June 2023, starting in Voru county in southeast Estonia. Tallinn first appears on the July 2024 list. It is the one clean case where the archive's record starts well before the naming.

Four FIRs were named while the archive saw them quiet

Southern Sweden, eastern Athens, Budapest and Bratislava joined the list in July 2024. The archive had covered each on hundreds of days before then and found no persistent interference. EASA's bulletin says its list drew on data from its network of analysts and open sources, which the archive cannot see.

Eleven areas showed interference that no named list included

Each showed persistent interference before February 2025, the last archived copy of the named list. Most first appear in EASA's earliest ranked table on record, in May 2025. That date marks EASA's change of method, so it gives no lag. Five of the eleven are Russian or Belarusian FIRs, and the lists do not say whether they meant to cover that airspace.

What this check is not

Both sides are related instruments, and EASA's method is only partly published, so neither one is ground truth. A lag here is the gap between two dates, and nothing more. FIR outlines come from the VATSIM flight-simulation community, not official charts. Nothing here says who causes any interference. The full method, every count and the registration are in the project repository under docs/EASA_STAGE0.md to docs/EASA_STAGE3.md.
